Three roles, three surfaces

Who can do what is set by physics and law, not by preference. That is what splits the app.
Inside facility tablet
Can: answer intake, learn, see status, approve what's said about them. Cannot: verify identity, receive mail, upload files, open accounts, browse the open web. Offline-first. Metered by the minute. One question per screen.
Family or friend phone, web
Can: receive mail, photograph the report, print and mail letters, take a call. Cannot: act without a signed, scoped authorization. Never asked to read or judge the report. One task on screen at a time. Long silences between tasks, by design.
Caseworker desktop
Can: triage, read the report, approve letters, hold the deadline, run 30 clients at once. Cannot: see more of the file than consent covers. Locked by default, not by policy memo. Work queue sorted by what expires first. Never a roster.
Build first Screen 09, triage. Six answers in, one of four states out, with the path attached. It is the only screen where the app does something a person could not do on paper, and the only one whose output can be scored for accuracy. Everything else is a list, a letter, or a status.
Then, in order 2. Intake, the six questions (tablet) 3. Letter draft and approval (desktop) 4. Family task screen and report upload 5. Case status timeline (tablet) Each one works standing alone. Stop anywhere and you still have a demo.
Verify before demo day Dispute and reinvestigation window, and what starts the clock Whether court fines, restitution and child support report, and when Free report entitlement and the mail-in route What actually triggers a bureau to escalate past rung 1 Which ID documents each bureau accepts at rung 2 Settled Sep 21: requirements vary by person. Some clear on name and SSN alone. POA is one accepted route, not the gate. Notary available at every facility law library. Primary sources only. This is the part that can go visibly wrong on stage.
The access ladder

What a bureau asks for varies per person and cannot be known in advance. So the app tries the cheapest rung first.

1. Plain request. Name, SSN, date of birth, address history, client's signature. For many people this is the whole story. 2. Identity documents. ID copy and proof of address, when a bureau cannot match the file. 3. Limited POA. Only when the helper has to act alone. Scoped to credit reports and disputes, twelve months, notarized at the law library, revocable from the tablet. 4. Program release. No outside helper at all. The org receives and acts under its own form. Never make everyone pay the cost of the hardest case. Forcing a notary trip on someone who would have cleared with a signature is how a tool gets abandoned at step one.

Assumed away, with the instructors' sign-off: facility and DOC approval, incumbent tablet vendor access, bureau data agreements, and funding for the counselor role. Say this on a slide rather than hoping nobody asks.

Not assumed away: a person inside cannot verify identity online, and a helper outside has no standing without a signed form. Those two shape the product, and no waiver makes them disappear.
