Triage, J. Whitfield

Six questions, one classification, one path. Built for a 30 to 45 minute session.
What we asked
Any account in your name, ever? Not that he knows of
Report came back as? No file found
Anything in collections? None found
Bank account right now? No
Court-ordered obligations? Restitution, amount unknown

Restitution routes to the obligations list, not the credit path. Different mechanism, different screen.

Where that lands him
1. No file. Credit invisible. This client
Path: builder loan or secured card, then one on-time payment stream. Fastest of the four. Roughly 6 to 9 months to a usable file, per the Cornish interview, Sep 21.
2. Thin or stale file Path: low-effort boosters first. Rent reporting, utility and phone reporting. Least work for the client, which is why it goes first.
3. Damaged file, real debt Path: debt triage before credit building, and set the expectation in years rather than months. Telling someone the truth here beats selling them a quick fix.
4. Errors on the report Path: dispute flow, screens 03 through 06. This is the only state with a legal clock attached, so it jumps the queue.

Design note: this classifier is the actual product. Everything else is letters and lists. It is also the only piece you can measure for accuracy, which is what makes it defensible in a demo.
